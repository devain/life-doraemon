# steelman: AI is going to make most knowledge workers irrelevant within a decade

_My objection: I work with AI daily and don't feel replaced; I feel multiplied._

---

## The view at its strongest

- I believe that in the same way industrial machines made most muscle work non-economic, AI is making most cognitive work non-economic — and the timeline is faster because software scales without retooling.
- I believe the work that remains is not "AI-augmented knowledge work" but a narrow band of taste-setting, relationship-building, and physical presence — and that band employs *orders of magnitude* fewer people than today's knowledge economy.
- I believe "feeling multiplied" is a phase, not an equilibrium. The first wave of any automation feels like a tool until the second wave realizes the tool only needs one operator per ten teams, then one per hundred.
- I believe the historical pattern — weavers were not "displaced gradually," they were displaced *suddenly* once the marginal economics flipped — applies here. Most people miss the cliff because they're standing on the plateau before it.

## The strongest case for it

- Coding assistants already 2–5× experienced developer output on specific tasks; junior tasks are increasingly fully automated. The labor-demand curve at the low end is bending.
- Customer service, junior legal work, copy-writing, basic analytics — sectors that employed millions are now mostly AI-handled in firms that have moved on it.
- The "augmentation, not replacement" framing is what every prior wave was sold as before it stopped being true. Excel didn't replace accountants until it did. Calculators didn't replace bookkeepers until they did.
- The 10-year horizon is not "current LLMs scaled up" — it's whatever comes *after* agents become reliably autonomous. The trend line on autonomy is steep, and the bottleneck is shrinking.

## The legitimate values it serves

Honesty about disruption. Concern for people whose plans assume continuity. Refusal to perform optimism that conceals real risk. Also: a value of *not lying to yourself* — the augmentation framing can be a form of denial, and the view-holder refuses that denial.

## Genuine weaknesses (still real, even in steelman form)

- "Within a decade" is doing heavy lifting. The mechanism (autonomous agents at human reliability across messy real-world domains) is not solved, and the demo-to-deployment gap in such domains has historically been longer than predicted.
- The view conflates "make irrelevant" with "displace economically." Many roles persist long after their economic value drops — regulation, trust, human preference all sustain them.
- Past automation waves created more jobs than they destroyed in aggregate. The view assumes this one is different. Plausible but not proven.

## What you'd have to learn or accept to find this compelling

You'd have to take seriously the timeline of *agentic* AI (not chat AI) reaching reliability across cross-domain work. If you think reliable agency is 25+ years away, the view is wrong by your own model. If you think it's 5–10 years away, the view becomes hard to escape — your "feeling multiplied" is the plateau, not the equilibrium.
